# Renaissance Capability Contract: Episteme

**Status:** Working contract v0.1  
**Authority:** Architecture  
**Relationship to canon:** Constrained by Renaissance constitutional and architectural documents. This contract does not override them.  
**Scope:** Renaissance-facing responsibility and boundary for Episteme.

---

## 1. Purpose

Episteme is a Renaissance capability/instrument for disciplined inquiry.

Its purpose within Renaissance is to help represent knowledge, expose meaningful unknowns, develop and compare explanatory possibilities, design discriminating investigations, preserve results, and make the resulting inquiry path inspectable.

Episteme is an instrument for inquiry, not an authority over truth.

## 2. Contractual boundary

The Renaissance-facing flow is:

```
external material
      |
      v
acquisition / capture
      |
      v
grounded representation
      |
      +--------------------+
      |                    |
      v                    v
interpretation         unknowns / gaps
      |                    |
      +---------+----------+
                |
                v
       hypotheses / predictions
                |
                v
        experiment design
                |
                v
             results
                |
                v
       explicit knowledge change
```

This is a responsibility model, not a requirement that these stages become separate services.

## 3. Renaissance expects Episteme to provide

### 3.1 Grounded representation

Episteme must preserve the distinction between externally attributable material and structures generated by the system.

Grounded records must retain appropriate provenance.

### 3.2 Provenance and lineage

Episteme must preserve enough lineage to answer, where applicable:

- where material came from;
- what was actually received or observed;
- what transformations occurred;
- what assumptions were introduced;
- what outputs resulted.

Provenance describes lineage. It does not certify truth.

### 3.3 Unknowns and gaps

Episteme must represent unresolved, unmeasured, contradictory, insufficiently evidenced, and unexplored states where the basis for treating them as gaps is itself inspectable.

Absence is not automatically a discovery.

### 3.4 Discovery

Episteme may generate:

- questions;
- gaps;
- tensions;
- candidate relationships;
- hypotheses;
- models;
- other interpretive structures.

Generated structures remain generated.

Passing through Episteme must not grant epistemic authority.

### 3.5 Prediction

When a hypothesis or model implies an observable consequence, Episteme should represent that prediction explicitly enough for it to be challenged or tested.

### 3.6 Investigation and experiment design

When competing explanations remain viable, Episteme should support identification of observations or experiments capable of distinguishing them.

Experiment proposals are not results.

### 3.7 Results

Episteme must preserve results, including failed predictions and unsuccessful experiments.

Results do not interpret themselves.

### 3.8 Explicit knowledge change

Evidence and results may change what remains supported, contradicted, unresolved, superseded, or uncertain.

Such changes must remain explicit and traceable rather than silently rewriting prior records.

### 3.9 Reproducibility and inspection

Where practical, Episteme should preserve the inputs, transformations, assumptions, outputs, and validation state necessary to reproduce or challenge an inquiry path.

Its public inspection surfaces should expose the existing underlying records rather than create a second epistemic representation.

## 4. Episteme does not own

Episteme does not own:

- Renaissance's constitutional purpose;
- Renaissance governance;
- human judgment;
- a universal definition of truth;
- the universal Renaissance data model;
- all discovery performed by Renaissance;
- all experiment execution;
- all domain knowledge;
- Organs runtime architecture;
- other projects' purposes;
- Renaissance canon.

Episteme may recommend, infer, expose, or generate. Those activities do not create constitutional or institutional authority.

## 5. Episteme's relationship to Organs

Organs supplies runtime infrastructure.

Episteme supplies domain capability.

The dependency is therefore conceptually:

```
ORGANS
runtime mechanisms
      |
      v
EPISTEME
inquiry capability
```

Organs mechanisms must not silently redefine Episteme's epistemic semantics.

Episteme must not silently redefine Organs' runtime responsibilities.

Where an interface is needed, the responsibility should be made explicit.

## 6. Episteme's relationship to other capabilities

Episteme may implement several conceptual capabilities together.

In particular, the following should not be split into separate Renaissance systems merely because they have distinct conceptual names:

- provenance;
- relationship mapping;
- unknown/gap handling;
- hypothesis formation;
- prediction;
- experiment design;
- contradiction analysis.

The correct boundary is responsibility, not the number of nouns in the architecture.

Another capability may later be extracted or introduced if implementation evidence demonstrates a genuine boundary.

## 7. Episteme's relationship to independent instruments

Renaissance may use or interoperate with independent instruments without absorbing them.

Episteme therefore does not become the mandatory integration point for:

- Tiger Den;
- AI Foundry;
- Behemoth;
- Leviathan;
- Namagiri;
- future independent projects.

Integration must be justified by an explicit contract.

## 8. Epistemic invariants

The following invariants apply at the Renaissance-facing boundary:

1. Processing is not proof.
2. Capture is not evidence.
3. Generation is not observation.
4. Discovery output is not automatically evidence.
5. A result does not interpret itself.
6. A contradiction is not silently resolved.
7. An unknown is not silently filled.
8. Provenance does not certify source truth.
9. Infrastructure does not acquire epistemic authority.
10. Episteme does not acquire authority merely because it is technically central.

## 9. Existing Episteme architecture

This contract does not require Episteme to discard its existing architecture.

The current Episteme implementation already provides substantial support for:

- external acquisition and capture;
- grounded records;
- provenance;
- relationships;
- evidence assessment;
- contradictions;
- discovery;
- unknowns and gaps;
- hypotheses;
- predictions;
- experiment design;
- results;
- execution lineage;
- public inspection.

These existing mechanisms should be evaluated against this contract rather than duplicated.

If an existing Episteme mechanism conflicts with Renaissance requirements, the mechanism may be changed.

## 10. Contract boundary versus implementation

This document specifies responsibility.

It does not yet prescribe:

- service decomposition;
- repository decomposition;
- universal schemas;
- universal APIs;
- deployment topology;
- implementation language;
- final integration protocol;
- complete autonomy policy.

Those remain architectural questions to be resolved by evidence and explicit decisions.

## 11. Acceptance condition

An Episteme implementation satisfies the Renaissance-facing contract only when it can demonstrate, through documentation and tests appropriate to the capability, that:

- epistemic distinctions survive the pipeline;
- provenance survives relevant transformations;
- generated material remains distinguishable from grounded material;
- unknowns and contradictions remain inspectable;
- experiments and results remain distinct;
- knowledge changes are explicit;
- public inspection does not silently mutate epistemic status;
- no runtime mechanism acquires authority merely through implementation.

The contract should be revised if implementation evidence demonstrates that any of these boundaries are inadequate.

## 12. Open questions

This contract intentionally leaves open:

- the final Renaissance-wide capability registry;
- formal capability discovery;
- cross-capability provenance exchange;
- universal identifiers;
- universal event semantics;
- formal interoperability protocol;
- how Renaissance should compose multiple discovery instruments;
- whether some Episteme responsibilities should eventually become independent capabilities;
- the boundary between computational discovery and scientific interpretation;
- the complete human review/governance process for consequential epistemic transitions.

These questions should not be answered merely for completeness. They should be resolved when a concrete requirement makes the boundary necessary.
