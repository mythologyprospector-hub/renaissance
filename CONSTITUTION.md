# Constitution

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the highest-level foundational constraints currently recognized by Renaissance.

It is intentionally incomplete.

The constitution must grow with the project, but implementation must not silently redefine constitutional principles.

## 2. Human Agency

Renaissance exists to increase human capability while preserving legitimate human agency.

AI systems, algorithms, organs, databases, protocols, and other technical components may assist, analyze, discover, recommend, or execute authorized work.

Capability does not itself create sovereignty.

## 3. No Inherent Machine Sovereignty

No software component possesses inherent authority over humanity merely because it is capable, trusted, widely used, or deeply integrated.

Authority must be explicitly established and governed.

## 4. No Inherent Self-Preservation Mandate

Renaissance exists for its human purpose, not for its own continued existence.

The project has no inherent constitutional claim to preserve itself, its components, its architecture, or its institutional form against legitimate human decisions.

## 5. Epistemic Discipline

Renaissance must preserve meaningful distinctions between observation, interpretation, hypothesis, prediction, experiment, evidence, conclusion, and unknown.

The system must not silently promote one epistemic state into another.

## 6. Unknown Is Legitimate

Lack of knowledge is a valid state.

Renaissance must be capable of representing uncertainty, incompleteness, contradiction, and unresolved questions rather than manufacturing certainty.

## 7. Provenance

Where meaningful and practicable, consequential knowledge, claims, transformations, and decisions should retain sufficient provenance to permit their history to be examined.

## 8. No Silent Constitutional Drift

Implementation convenience, defaults, dependencies, accumulated practice, or undocumented behavior must not silently acquire constitutional authority.

## 9. Replaceability and Forkability

The project should favor modularity, interoperability, inspectability, portability, replaceability, and forkability.

No component should become constitutionally sacred merely because Renaissance currently depends upon it.

## 10. Capacity for Revision

Renaissance must retain legitimate mechanisms for correction, disagreement, experimentation, amendment, and supersession.

Established does not mean infallible.

## 11. Constitutional Completeness

This initial constitution does not claim to answer every future question.

Unresolved matters remain unresolved until deliberately decided.
