# Governance

## Current phase

Renaissance is in its founding phase.

The project's governance model is therefore intentionally incomplete.

The current Human Gate is the final authority for foundational decisions until a more developed and explicitly adopted governance structure supersedes that arrangement.

## Authority Hierarchy

Renaissance recognizes the following order of authority:

1. **Constitutional Canon**
2. **Foundational Canon established by the Charter and ratified Decisions**
3. **Established requirements and architecture**
4. **Implementation**
5. **Proposals and experiments**

Lower-level material may implement, interpret, or explore higher-level material, but may not silently override it.

If lower-level material conflicts with higher-level canon, the conflict must be resolved through the change-control process rather than by silently changing the higher-level rule.

Implementation details do not acquire constitutional authority merely through use, age, or widespread adoption.

## Roles

### Human Gate

Provides human judgment, intent, approval, and final authorization for foundational decisions during the founding phase.

### Architect

Develops architecture, standards, decomposition, coordination, review, and proposals.

The Architect may recommend and document decisions but does not acquire constitutional authority merely by performing these functions.

### Builder

Implements authorized work, tests it, and reports results.

A Builder does not silently redesign constitutional or architectural rules while implementing a mission.

### Contributor

May provide research, code, documentation, criticism, proposals, experiments, or other useful work.

Future contributor roles and authority are intentionally left open.

## Future governance

As the project grows, governance may be formalized further.

Any such change must be explicitly documented and adopted rather than emerging accidentally from technical practice.
