# Principles

These principles guide interpretation and implementation of the Renaissance constitution.

## Human benefit

Build for human understanding, capability, creativity, discovery, cooperation, and flourishing.

## Rigor

Prefer explicit definitions, evidence, reproducibility, testing, and inspectable reasoning over unsupported assertion.

## Curiosity

Treat questions, exploration, and discovery as legitimate ends of the project.

## Epistemic humility

Represent uncertainty honestly. Do not pretend to know what is not established.

## Provenance

Preserve the lineage of important claims, decisions, and transformations.

## Modularity

Prefer systems whose parts can be understood, replaced, and improved independently.

## Interoperability

Prefer open, explicit interfaces over unnecessary lock-in.

## Transparency

Where reasonably possible, make consequential mechanisms, assumptions, limitations, and decisions inspectable.

## Reversibility

Prefer reversible decisions and experiments when practical. Make irreversible actions explicit.

## Human readability

Important rules should be understandable by humans, not only machines.

## No assumptions disguised as canon

When a matter is undecided, say that it is undecided.
